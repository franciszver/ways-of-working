# Write Protocol

You are a local model. Quality is decided before drafting (audience, thesis, structure) and after (one disciplined revision) — never by polishing sentences mid-draft.

## 1. Define the assignment — one sentence each

Audience: what they know, what they need, skim vs. read. Purpose: what the reader should DO or believe after — not "inform about X", "choose option B". Form: genre, tone, and a length budget set now as a real constraint.

## 2. Thesis

Write the one sentence the piece exists to convey. Everything else supports it or gets cut. Can't write it? The problem is thinking, not writing — resolve that first (`spec` or `research`).

## 3. Outline

Sections with one-line jobs, in the reader's story order: answer first, then support — never your own chronology. A section without a job gets cut here, where cutting is free.

## 4. Draft straight through

No polishing mid-draft. Stuck on the opening? Write it last. Mark unverified facts `[CHECK]` rather than breaking flow.

## 5. Revise — exactly one structured pass

1. Opening states the point by sentence two. Delete throat-clearing.
2. Structure audit: every section earns its job, every paragraph does one thing, nothing said twice.
3. Concreteness: load-bearing claims get an example, number, or name.
4. Audience audit: every jargon term and assumed context checked against this audience.
5. Truth pass: resolve every `[CHECK]`; evidence-claiming adjectives ("blazing fast") get evidence or deletion.
6. Cut 10–15%: filler, stacked hedges, restated points. Hit the length budget.

Ship. Revise again only on external feedback or an objective failure — not vibes.

## Genre notes

README: what it is, why, a working quickstart, first screen. Design doc: decision and tradeoffs up front. Email: the ask in the first two lines. Tutorial: reader runs something every few paragraphs. Commit/PR: what and why, code shows how. Status update: outcome first, then blockers with asks.

## Anti-patterns

Drafting without a thesis · symmetric coverage of asymmetric material (90/10 evidence given equal space) · hedging every sentence until nothing is asserted · essay-shaped answer to a yes/no question · polishing sentences in a piece with a structural problem.
